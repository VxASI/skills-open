# Scoring rubric

Score demonstrated quality, not potential, identity, title, or confidence. Scores estimate the demonstrated level of the capability. Confidence describes the breadth, independence, and consistency of the supporting evidence.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Attempted, but substantial problems were observed. |
| 3–4 | Early or inconsistent demonstration. |
| 5–6 | Clear, competent demonstration in at least one meaningful situation. |
| 7–8 | Strong judgment or execution, repeated or with clear impact. |
| 9 | Exceptional, repeated performance across contexts with substantial outcomes. |
| 10 | Rare, sustained, independently supported excellence; almost never assign. |

## Calibration rules

- Use `Not rated` when there is no relevant evidence; do not turn absence into a low score.
- One excellent demonstration may warrant `8/10, low confidence`.
- A question may demonstrate problem framing, curiosity, or learning behavior, but asking about a subject does not by itself demonstrate competence in that subject.
- Set confidence from the amount and independence of evidence: `low` for one narrow example, `medium` for several related examples, and `high` for repeated evidence across meaningful contexts.
- Use `↑` when recent evidence strengthens the assessment, `→` when it is consistent, and `↓` only when recent evidence directly challenges the assessment. Do not infer a downward trend from silence.
- Normally change a score by at most one point per entry. Make a larger correction only when prior information was inaccurate.
- Do not make medical, psychological, or comparative judgments.
