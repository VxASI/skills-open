# Comparison mode

Read this reference only when the user asks to compare two public professional profiles, asks how similar two people are professionally, wants to understand overlap, or wants to evaluate a credible collaboration angle.

Do not assume comparison against the user. If comparison was not requested, the agent may offer it once after the main profile:

`Want me to compare this profile against you or someone else?`

## Research parity

Before scoring:

1. Resolve both identities independently.
2. Research both profiles to a comparable evidence depth.
3. Lower confidence or gather more evidence if one profile is much thinner than the other.
4. Use public professional evidence only.

## Comparison scores

Use up to three distinct scores because they answer different questions.

### Professional Overlap

How much the two public professional graphs intersect.

Consider:

- domains;
- problems explored;
- technologies;
- products and projects;
- communities and ecosystems;
- audiences;
- current interests.

### Similarity

How alike the profiles are in demonstrated work, professional interests, role shape, and current direction.

Similarity is not a personality score and must not rely on inferred private traits.

### Collaboration Fit

Whether shared interests plus complementary strengths create a credible reason to work, talk, research, invest, hire, or collaborate together.

High similarity does not automatically mean high collaboration fit. Complementary profiles can have high collaboration fit with only moderate similarity.

## Evidence contract

For every comparison score include:

- score from 0–100;
- confidence;
- strongest supporting overlaps;
- strongest meaningful differences;
- evidence behind any claimed complementarity.

When useful, also include:

- **Shared graph:** topics, projects, technologies, organizations, or ecosystems that overlap;
- **Complementary graph:** credible differences that may make the connection useful;
- **Best connection angle:** one evidence-backed reason the two people may benefit from a conversation;
- **Conversation starters:** only when requested or clearly useful.

Do not manufacture a connection merely to increase the score.

## Public professional connections

A comparison or rich report may show a connection graph. Include only connections supported by public professional evidence, such as:

- person → company;
- person → project;
- person → publication;
- person → public alias or brand;
- person → coauthor or collaborator;
- person → event, interview, investment, or organization when the relationship is publicly established.

Label inferred connections as inferred. Do not imply friendship, endorsement, employment, investment, partnership, or collaboration without evidence.
